# Meaning beyond dictionary definitions

This Chinese-to-English example illustrates a principle that applies to any source language.

**User:** 我没那么多精神去看。

An initial suggestion, “I don’t have the mental energy to read through it,” captures tiredness or limited mental capacity, but may miss the intended attitude.

**User clarification:** 我没空去看，没时间或者没精力，也觉得有点浪费时间，就是烦烦的。

The meaning includes reluctance, annoyance, and a sense that reading it is not worth the effort—not just exhaustion. Possible expressions emphasize different parts:

- **“I can’t really be bothered to read through all that.”** Reluctance to spend the effort; conversational, especially in British English, but blunt and potentially dismissive.
- **“I don’t have the bandwidth to go through all that right now.”** Limited time or mental capacity; softer, common in work contexts.
- **“Reading through all that feels like a chore.”** The reading feels tedious or burdensome.
- **“I don’t think it’s worth spending that much time on.”** The time investment does not feel worthwhile.

For the user's request to keep the skill short:

> Keep the skill short—I can’t really be bothered to read through a long set of instructions.

These are contextual alternatives, not interchangeable translations of 精神. The user's explanation helps identify the right emphasis; a dictionary definition alone cannot settle it.
